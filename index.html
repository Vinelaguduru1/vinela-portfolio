One cart for every vehicle change

A simplified illustration of the idea, not the production interface.

THE PROBLEM

Customers who needed to change the vehicles on their policy were confused about how. Adding, replacing, and removing a vehicle are three different jobs, but customers just think "my car changed."

WHO IT WAS FOR

People who need insurance for their vehicles. They are not insurance experts and usually arrive after buying, selling, or replacing a car.

HOW I LEARNED

I worked with users directly to learn where the experience confused them and what it was missing, and used that to decide what the cart had to make obvious.

THE APPROACH

  • Treat add, replace, and remove as clear, named choices inside one cart.
  • Show each pending change as a line item so people can review before confirming.
  • Keep the language plain and the steps few.

Recreated concept screens with sample data. They illustrate the flow and are not the production Liberty Mutual interface.

OUTCOME

After launch, customers could reach and complete vehicle changes more easily. Next time I would set completion and drop-off targets before launch so the improvement can be measured.

THE PROBLEM

Customers move between managing their policy, paying a bill, and servicing their account. These areas were built over time and by different teams, and customers expect them to feel like one product.

THE APPROACH

  • Define reusable components that behave the same everywhere, and work with engineers to build them.
  • Set shared patterns so policy, billing, and servicing screens follow the same rules.
  • Use reviews to teach the patterns to junior teammates.
  • Use production monitoring (Datadog, Splunk, CloudWatch) to catch problems that hurt the customer experience.
